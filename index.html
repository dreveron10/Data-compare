              How the numbers are made

              Everything runs in your browser. Flight files, videos and the library never leave this computer unless you export or share them.

              What it reads

              Blue Raven high-rate (500 Hz) and low-rate (50 Hz) CSVs, the flight summary and the deployment settings workbook. Featherweight GPS logs and summaries. OpenRocket CSV exports for comparison. Other altimeters (Eggtimer, RRC3, StratoLogger, Altus Metrum and any CSV with a time and an altitude column) as comparisons: columns are found by name, units from the header. Files are recognised by their columns, not their names.

              Altitude and apogee

              Baro altitude is filtered with a 1 s running median and then a 0.2 s moving average, so ejection-charge pressure spikes don't move the curve. AGL is that altitude minus the pad altitude from the summary. Apogee is the filtered peak, searched only within 2 s of the inertial apogee so a spike can't be mistaken for it.

              Events and deployments

              Fire times come from the summary when it has them, then from the channel continuity dropping below 1 V, then from the low-rate fired flag (which lags the real fire by about 1.4 s). Deployment conditions are checked in the 0.15 s before each fire.

              Attitude and the 3D replay

              The Blue Raven quaternions are relative to the pad. The rocket axis comes from the summary's direction of first motion. Heading is found by matching the rocket's azimuth to the inertial velocity during ascent; both quaternion conventions and both crossrange signs are tried and the best fit wins. Roll is shown about the rocket axis.

              GPS alignment

              The GPS clock is matched to the Blue Raven by cross-matching vertical velocity over plus or minus 3 s (you can override the offset). The Blue Raven track is rotated onto true east and north by least squares. On the 3D path the ascent is blended so it meets the GPS position at apogee, and the descent uses GPS position directly. Launch time comes from the GPS UTC column.

              Recovery and descent

              Kinetic energy is ½·(m/32.174)·v² in ft-lbf, using the section masses you enter. Parachute CdA is 2W/(ρv²), with air density from the logged pressure and temperature. Wind is the GPS horizontal drift under a settled parachute, binned by altitude, skipping the first 4 s after each deployment.

              Comparisons

              Sims and other flights are lined up at liftoff, apogee or main deploy, then shifted by the time you set. Sim acceleration is shown the way an accelerometer would read it (kinematic plus 1 g).

              Mach and dynamic pressure

              Speed is the vertical velocity divided by the cosine of the tilt, because the inertial horizontal velocity drifts. Air density comes from the logged pressure and a standard lapse-rate temperature from the pad. q = ½ρv², Mach = v / √(γRT).

              Coast drag

              Between burnout and apogee, Cd = 2·m·a / (ρ·v²·A), with the burnout mass and body diameter from the Vehicle card. The window stops at any pyro fire before apogee so an early ejection doesn't count as drag. The sim hint fits a quadratic-drag coast to the sim's own coast and asks how high it would go from the real burnout speed.

              Motor, rail exit and liftoff

              Thrust = m·a + drag (coast Cd, or 0.45 without one), with mass falling in step with delivered impulse (constant Isp), solved iteratively. Rail exit integrates the accelerometer reading minus 1 g from first motion (above 1.15 g) until the rocket has moved the rail length, then compares to the 52 ft/s Student Launch minimum. Angle of attack at rail exit is the arctangent of the lowest GPS wind over rail exit speed.

              Ejection timing

              For each pyro fire, the first jolt in the accelerometer after it (the charge separating the airframe) and the biggest load in the next 6 s (the parachute opening), stopping before touchdown.

              Student Launch checks

              Rail exit speed, kinetic energy of each section under the main, descent time, drift and apogee against the declared target, each against the handbook limit. Masses come from the Vehicle card, so placeholder masses give placeholder results.

              Parachute sizing and landing what-if

              Diameter for a target rate: D = √(4·2W / (ρv²Cd) / π). The what-if starts at the point where the flown chute had settled, then descends through this flight's GPS wind profile with the new main altitude or drogue rate.

              Fin flutter

              Flutter speed from Peak of Flight #291 (based on NACA TN 4197): Vf = a·√(G / (1.337·AR³·P·(λ+1) / (2(AR+2)(t/c)³))), using local speed of sound and pressure at each point of the flight. The margin is the smallest ratio of flutter speed to speed flown. It is a screening number for flat, solid fins.

              Library

              Saved flights live in this browser's storage. Deleted flights go to the trash for 30 days, then are removed for good. Trends, health (battery and data warnings) and the flight log use the numbers stored when each flight was saved, so re-save older flights after changing the Vehicle card. An OpenRocket .ork can fill the Vehicle card: largest body tube diameter, first trapezoidal fin set and the first simulation's mass at liftoff and burnout.

              Earth view

              Esri World Imagery draped on AWS Terrain Tiles (Terrarium elevation), loaded in rings from about 1 m per pixel near the pad out to a few km per pixel at the horizon, with Earth curvature.

              Findings are rules of thumb, not verdicts. Check anything that matters against the raw logs.
